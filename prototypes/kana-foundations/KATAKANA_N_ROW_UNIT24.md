# Kana Foundations Unit 24 — Katakana N-row (isolated)

## Curriculum continuity and teaching
Units 01–19 developed hiragana recognition and integrated reading; Unit 20 introduced katakana vowels, Unit 21 K-row, Unit 22 S-row, and Unit 23 T-row. Unit 24 introduces ナ na / ニ ni / ヌ nu / ネ ne / ノ no, corresponding to hiragana な・に・ぬ・ね・の. The standalone nasal ン is NOT an N-row kana and is not taught or assessed. The N-row completes the first 25 basic katakana from vowels through N-row. No ハ row, long vowel bar, small ッ, voiced katakana, or other untaught characters assessed.

## Independent evidence curriculum
Stage A has 15 first prompts: five explicit teach-hide-recall items, five five-choice recognition items, three complete word readings ネコ (cat), イヌ (dog), ナス (eggplant), and two script contrasts. A saved checkpoint needs explicit acknowledgment.
Stage B has 16: seven fully decodable readings キノコ (mushroom), キツネ (fox), タヌキ (Japanese raccoon dog), カニ (crab), ナシ (pear), ネタ (sushi topping or story material), タネ (seed); five N-row hiragana counterparts; four unambiguous contextual meaning choices. All 10 assessed words use only the 25 katakana of Units 20–24. All five new katakana appear in at least one fully assessed word.
Earlier standard romanization equivalences remain accepted when a word actually uses シ or ツ: キツネ / kitsune / kitune, ナシ / nashi / nasi. No arbitrary transcription shortcuts are accepted.

## Accurate word usage and provenance
Animals/plants commonly take katakana. ネコ, イヌ, ナス, ナシ are explicitly found in Rafael E. Beermann, Intensive Japanese I, Katakana Handbook, University of Duisburg-Essen, p. 5, Exercise 6, and the standard table places the N-row after the T-row. https://www.uni-due.de/imperia/md/content/japan/katakana_handbook.pdf
タヌキ refers to the Japanese raccoon dog (not a raccoon), see Shogakukan Digital Daijisen, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%8B%B8-562006.
ナシ means pear (梨) in a produce context and can refer to none (無し) elsewhere. ネタ is a sushi ingredient/topping or story material by context; タネ means a seed. These distinctions are explicit in learner instructions and assessed questions. For common Japanese words, katakana presentation should never imply that hiragana/kanji spellings are invalid.
